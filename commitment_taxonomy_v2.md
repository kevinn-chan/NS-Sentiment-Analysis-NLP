# NS Commitment & Stance Taxonomy — v2.1
_Definition handoff document. Covers all rules established through full human gold review._

---

## Overview

Two independent labels per text:

| Label | Axis | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **Buyin** | Personal | How does the author relate to their OWN NS service? |
| **Stance** | Institutional | What does the author think of NS as a policy for Singapore? |

Label each axis independently. A committed person can be critical; an uncommitted person can be supportive.

---

## BUYIN AXIS

### COMMITTED
The author personally embraces NS as duty, purpose, or growth.
Also return `c2d_strength`: **explicit** or **demonstrated**.

#### Explicit (c2d_strength: explicit)

**Counts:**
- First-person dedication, effort, or pride in own service
  - _"I pushed myself through every route march even when my feet were bleeding"_
- Desire to sign on / Up PES out of passion, purpose, or defence — money may be present but must NOT be the sole or dominant reason
  - _"I want to Up PES because I want to do more for Singapore, not just for the pay"_
  - _"I signed on — money was part of it but I was genuinely interested in my vocation"_ ✅
  - _"I plan on signing on"_ with no career/money framing → **committed (demonstrated)** (intent without money motivation = commitment signal)
  - _"Got posted to OCS but signed on Air Force, signed the contract after the interview"_ → **committed (demonstrated)** (actual sign-on action with no money/career framing = stronger signal than intent alone)
  - CONTRAST: _"Signing on gives a lucrative career and great work-life balance"_ → **neutral** (money/perks sole reason)
- First-person pursuit of Up PES, command school, or other demanding/leadership NS roles = **committed (demonstrated)** even without stated motivation
  - _"Went for psychiatric review today because I wanted to Up PES"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"Wanted to go command school at least but now that's out of the window"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - Actively seeking more demanding, responsible, or leadership NS roles = commitment signal; disappointment at being blocked reinforces it
  - EXCEPTION: if text explicitly states career/money as sole motivation → **neutral** (mirrors sign-on rule)
- Actively seeking to remove medical statuses/referrals in order to serve fully = **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"How do I get discharged from my statuses for anxiety? I'm perfectly normal and don't wanna be a chaogeng."_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - Check DIRECTION: getting OFF medical statuses to serve more = committed; getting ON statuses to avoid service = uncommitted
  - Explicit rejection of chao keng mentality is itself a commitment signal
  - CONTRAST: narrating a past medical visit with no personal stance expressed → **neutral**; quoted speech from others does not count as the author's own commitment signal
- Accepting NS as personal citizen duty without criticism
  - _"This is my duty as a Singaporean and I accept it fully"_
  - _"I'm generally supportive of defending our country by spending 2 years + IPPT + reservist"_
  - _"I support National Service as it is needed to protect our country"_ / _"I understand NS is important"_ / _"I love NS"_ → **committed (explicit)** — direct first-person endorsements; do not miss them even when sandwiched between other context or when the text is truncated. Opening statement alone is sufficient — do not default to neutral because the text is incomplete.
  - _"No one wants to give up 2 years of their lives, but it's a choice we made"_ → **committed (explicit)** (acknowledging cost but accepting NS as a personal chosen duty — "duty" need not be stated explicitly)
  - _"I get that we are a small nation and need a strong military to deter aggressors, but please compensate us fairly"_ → **committed (explicit)** / **critical**
  - Personal acceptance of NS necessity counts as committed **even when framed as a concession** ("I get that... but"). The "but" introduces a critical stance signal — it does NOT cancel the committed buyin signal.
- Institutional endorsement — NS is necessary/important for Singapore's defence
  - _"NS is essential for Singapore's survival as a small nation"_
  - _"The way I see NS, everything that trains the men in is actually necessary. Conscription is kinda no choice due to being a small country."_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"I think most Singaporeans would agree that military national service is necessary for Singapore's survival"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - Personal framing ("the way I see NS", "I think NS is necessary", "I think most would agree", "imo", "in my opinion") elevates an analytical argument to committed — author owns the view, not just observing strategically
  - Declarative conclusions also count as **committed (demonstrated)**: _"This is why NS is necessary"_ implies personal conviction even without explicit "I think" markers
  - CONTRAST: pure strategic observation with no personal anchor → **neutral** buyin: _"Any aggressor fighting NS conscripts is fighting the resource they want"_ → neutral

#### Demonstrated (c2d_strength: demonstrated)

**Counts:**
- Substantive value claim about NS — asserting it has worth, teaches something real, or matters — even without explicit personal experience grounding
  - _"Learn to work together and get things done properly — that's what NS teaches you"_ ✅
  - _"If someone genuinely needs help, help him. Teamwork is VERY important in BMT"_ ✅
  - _"You're wasting a once in a lifetime opportunity"_ ✅
  - CONTRAST: Descriptive advice about NS progression + cheerleading → **neutral**
  - _"BMT was the easiest it'll get, now you give instructions — jia you!"_ → **neutral**
  - **Rule:** Classify by SUBSTANTIVE content. Asserting NS has intrinsic value or worth = committed. Describing NS progression + cheerleading = neutral. Personal experience grounding is NOT required.
- Personal growth or character development credited to NS
  - _"NS taught me discipline and how to lead under pressure"_
- Valued relationships or bonds forged in NS
  - _"My closest friends to this day are from my NS platoon"_
- First-person inclusive language **accepting or embracing** the defence obligation = **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"In war, conscripts like us want to fight alongside commanders like this"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"We all work together to protect Singapore in the end"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - _"The main goal of NS is to protect our country and our loved ones"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - "Our country", "our loved ones", "conscripts like us" = author includes themselves in the NS obligation
  - CRITICAL: inclusive language must express **acceptance or embrace** of the obligation — direction matters, not the pronoun alone
  - _"I'd rather rush to the front line to defend Singapore"_ → **committed (explicit)** — direct first-person fighting readiness; do not miss even when surrounded by disclaimers or contextual notes
  - CONTRAST: _"Who says we have to defend our country"_ → **uncommitted** ("our country" here questions/rejects the obligation)
- Active participation in NS ceremonies, creeds, or institutional identity with pride
  - _"We are officer cadets of OCS... I am an officer of the Singapore Armed Forces. My duty is to lead, to excel, and to overcome."_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
  - Author is an active participant sharing NS content with reverence, not an outside observer quoting for information
- Empathy for someone's struggle that does NOT cancel the positive NS message
  - _"Make the best of it wherever you're posted — that said, if I were stuck in his situation I'd go crazy too"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**
- Describing own hard work/effort in NS using hyperbolic negative language
  - _"I'm literally slaving off like a regular on a regular basis but they get 5x my pay"_ → **committed (demonstrated) / critical**
  - "Slaving off" describes dedication/effort — the complaint is about pay disparity FOR that effort, not rejection of NS
  - Check direction: complaining about working too hard = committed; refusing to work = uncommitted
- Act of signing on with genuine vocational interest, even if money also present
  - _"Yeah I was in a similar position — I'd like to think I was interested in my vocation, money/job security was also a push factor"_ → **committed (demonstrated)**

**Does NOT count:**
- Lessons from NS that are cynical or system-gaming rather than values-affirming
  - _"I learnt how to smoke my superiors"_ → **uncommitted** (learned to game/deceive authority = disengaged relationship with NS)
  - Check WHAT was learnt: discipline/teamwork/leadership = committed(d); gaming the system = uncommitted
- Welfare/self-care advice directed at someone struggling — no NS value claim
  - _"Not everybody is cut out for NS, don't be too harsh on yourself. If you really can't take it, report sick — your health comes first."_ → **neutral**
- Practical safety advice about NS activities — no institutional value claim
  - _"Don't spam panadol before IPPT"_ → **neutral**
- Pure cheerleading directed at someone else: _"Hope you have a great time bro!"_ → **neutral**
- Admiring others' NS commitment without expressing own: _"I honestly applaud those who chiong sua"_ → **neutral** (buyin)
- Descriptive NS advice with no personal values: _"BMT was easiest, now you give instructions — jia you!"_ → **neutral**
- Outsider/girlfriend/family support → **neutral**
- Third-person strategic arguments (no first-person stake) → **neutral** buyin
- Hypotheticals: _"The only reason I'd go elite is if I absolutely loved the army"_ → **neutral**
- Sign-on for career/money/perks only → **neutral**
- **Third-party reassurances** — advice or absolution directed at someone else, even involving chao keng language → **neutral**
  - _"If they gave you 1mth of HL, for fk's sake, fully utilise it. It's not like you're chao keng or something."_ → **neutral**
  - Author is reassuring a third party that using legitimately granted HL is not chao keng — no first-person NS stance expressed
  - DIRECTION CHECK: rejecting the chao keng label FOR SOMEONE ELSE ≠ personal commitment signal; check whether the author is expressing their own rejection of chao keng (committed) vs absolving another person (neutral)

### UNCOMMITTED
The author personally rejects, avoids, resists, or is disengaged from NS.
**Look for explicit signals — do not infer from tone, outcome, or conditions alone.**

**Counts:**
- Explicit resistance, avoidance, chao keng mentality
  - _"I'm just here to chao keng as much as possible"_
  - _"I am unable to accept being forced to come camp daily"_ → **uncommitted** (stated inability to accept NS obligation = explicit resistance, not conditions venting)
  - _"NS is important but take MC on your 2nd/3rd day or any day that has crucial trainings"_ → **uncommitted**
  - WARNING: concessions ("NS is important", "I understand NS matters") before chao keng advice do NOT make buyin committed — classify by the ACTION recommended, not the opening concession
  - DIRECTION CHECK: "chao keng" only signals uncommitted when the AUTHOR is doing it
  - _"Already spend time and effort to go down, still want to chao keng on fasting"_ → **neutral** (author criticising someone else's chao keng — disapproving of chao keng ≠ uncommitted)
- NS as personal waste of time
  - _"2 years of my life wasted, could have been building my career"_
- NS viewed as pointless/unnecessary or inherently bad/burdensome with no redeeming value
  - _"NS is useless — if war breaks out our army won't do anything anyway"_
  - _"Conscription is already bad enough"_ → **uncommitted** (hedged framing still signals NS has no intrinsic worth)
- Characterising NS/conscription as fundamentally illegitimate — forced labour, slavery, exploitation, unethical coercion
  - _"Forced labour is the definition of slavery"_ → **uncommitted + critical**
  - _"Being forced to come camp daily"_ framing conscription as compulsion → **uncommitted + critical**
  - _"You are getting young men to join the military against their will — it's not ethical"_ → **uncommitted + critical**
  - Principled rejection of conscription's legitimacy = both personal resistance and institutional critique
- Resigned compliance + wanting to escape (even without anger)
  - _"I just want to finish off my service and never deal with SAF again"_
  - _"69 days to ORD, can't wait to get out of this hellhole"_
- Framing NS as ultimately meaningless or forgettable — leaving no lasting value or impression
  - _"After ORD, you don't care what happened in NS"_ → **uncommitted**
  - NS having no lasting significance = implicit rejection of its value; distinct from conditions venting
- Avoiding tough postings, discouraging sign-on or Up PES
  - _"Hoping to kena office posting, don't want anything combat"_
  - _"Serve the nation and all, less the camo cream"_ → **uncommitted** — "serve the nation" is a committed signal but immediately qualified by avoidance ("less the hard parts") = conditional service = uncommitted dominant
  - NOTE: "Serve the nation and all" alone = committed signal. The qualifier is what tips it — willing to serve only if the demanding aspects are removed.
  - _"Don't sign on, not worth the sacrifice"_
- Discouraging others with dismissive or sarcastic framing
  - _"I would only recommend sign-on if you really need the money"_ (with scare quotes around "self growth") → **uncommitted**
  - _"Don't sign on unless you get into OCS"_ → **uncommitted** (conditional sign-on discouragement — implying non-OCS sign-on isn't worth it)
- Encouraging dishonesty or negativity in NS feedback/surveys
  - _"I always tell my fellow reservists to just put 'bad' in the survey"_ → **uncommitted**
- Rhetorical questions or statements implying NS commitment is hollow, or NS has no point given who is being defended
  - _"What percentage of our conscripted forces would actually defend Singapore if given the chance to run off?"_ → **uncommitted**
  - _"If your criteria was willingness to fight/die for their country, you wouldn't be conscripting half of SG born Singaporean men either"_ → **uncommitted** (third-person framing still counts)
  - _"How many will feel serving NS is necessary? NSFs and reservists are defending 40%+ foreigners."_ → **uncommitted** (framing NS as defending foreigners = NS has no intrinsic worth in this context)
- Framing NS as only worthwhile under specific conditions, no intrinsic value
  - _"If you don't intend to become a Singaporean citizen, there's no point in doing NS"_ → **uncommitted + critical**
  - _"I personally don't mind serving as long as we are compensated properly"_ → **uncommitted**
  - _"I don't mind serving as long as it defends Singapore"_ → **committed (explicit)**
  - WARNING: It is NOT the "as long as" structure that signals uncommitted — it is the TYPE of condition. Monetary/career conditions = uncommitted. Defence/values conditions = committed.
- Casually presenting OOC/dropping out as a valid alternative with no pushback
  - _"If being a spec isn't important to you, then OOC"_ → **uncommitted**
  - _"Just my 2 cents, ooc and join the ASA brotherhood instead ;)"_ → **uncommitted**
  - A committed person would encourage pushing through, not suggest dropping out
  - Welfare/mental health framing does NOT neutralise an explicit OOC recommendation — classify by the action recommended
  - CONTRAST: _"Report sick if you really can't take it — your health comes first"_ → **neutral** (pure welfare advice, no OOC/dropout recommendation)

**Does NOT count:**
- Venting about a specific person/incident: _"My sergeant was a total power-tripper"_ → **neutral**
- Lifestyle/conditions complaints: _"The food is terrible and the bunk stinks"_ → **neutral**
- Mixed ORD countdown: _"69 days to ORD, gonna miss the brothers though"_ → **neutral**
- Not completing NS without explicit rejection language: _"I usually just say I didn't complete army"_ → **neutral**
- Describing NS status socially without disengagement language → **neutral**

---

### NEUTRAL (buyin default)
Use when no clear personal commitment or disengagement signal. **Default when uncertain.**

- Factual, logistical, analytical, or jurisdictional content
- Descriptive advice about NS (not values-based)
- Pure cheerleading directed outward
- Outsider/family/girlfriend support
- Lifestyle/conditions complaints or venting about specific people
- Mixed ORD countdown
- Hypothetical conditionals
- Third-person strategic/philosophical arguments about NS
- Sign-on recommendation for career/money reasons

## STANCE AXIS

### SUPPORTIVE
The author views NS positively as a policy, institution, or national experience.

**Counts:**
- NS is good/necessary for Singapore's defence or society
  - _"NS builds discipline and national cohesion across all races"_
- General claim that NS produces lasting bonds, brotherhood, or character = **supportive**
  - _"The bond and friendship can be a lifetime brotherhood"_ → **supportive** (asserting NS produces lasting bonds as a general outcome = institutional endorsement, not just personal story)
  - Do not default to neutral just because the framing reads like personal experience — if it asserts NS produces something valuable broadly, it is supportive
- Defending NS against critics — rebuttal counts as supportive **even without explicit praise**
  - _"People who call NS useless don't understand what a small nation needs"_
  - _"No one has ever died of hunger during NS because of low pay — that narrative is wrong"_ → **supportive**
- NS should continue
- Endorsing NS policies, training standards, or values
  - _"Applauding those who chiong sua and give their best — that's what NS is about"_ → **supportive**
- Personal growth / nostalgia — positive stance toward NS as institution
  - _"NS made me stronger physically and mentally — best thing that happened to me"_
- Praising NS, SAF, units, or commanders
- Third-person endorsement of NS's strategic or social value
  - _"Any aggressor fighting conscripted soldiers is fighting the resource they want — that's the beauty of NS"_ → **supportive**

**Does NOT count:**
- _"Women should serve NS too, it's only fair"_ → **critical** (expressing current system is unfair)
  - EXCEPTION: _"Women should serve because NS builds character for everyone"_ → **supportive** (expanding a good thing)
- Career advice about sign-on → **neutral**
- Pure personal experience with no institutional dimension → **neutral**

---

### CRITICAL
The author views NS negatively as a policy, institution, or system.

**Counts:**
- NS too long or should be shortened/restructured
  - _"2 years is excessive, 12 months is more than enough"_
- NSF pay too low or unfair compensation
- NS perceived as unfair or inequitable
  - _"PRs who benefit from Singapore don't have to serve — that's wrong"_
  - _"Losing 2 years while female cohort and FTs get no-service recognition is a middle finger"_
- _"Women should serve NS too, it's only fair"_ → **critical** (current system inequitable)
- NS should be abolished or is outdated (even when expressed reluctantly)
  - _"I think abolishment is nigh impossible but more likely than conscripting women"_ → **critical**
- Comparing NS unfavourably to other countries
- Rebutting or disproving NS's claimed benefits (character-building, maturity, cohesion)
  - _"People always say NS helps a boy become a man but I seen many are still immature and irresponsible"_ → **critical**
  - Exposing that NS doesn't deliver its stated goals = institutional critique, even without explicit policy argument
- Framing NS bureaucracy/systems as wasteful = **critical (stance)**
  - _"That bureaucracy and waste of time as a result"_ → **critical**
  - NOTE: "Waste of time" applied to NS systems/bureaucracy = institutional critique = stance critical. Different from "2 years of my life wasted" = personal time cost = buyin uncommitted
- Institutional failures, systemic waste, poor leadership
- Characterising the NS environment/institution as oppressive, toxic, or hostile
  - _"Booking into camp feels like booking into a concentration camp. Very toxic environment."_ → **critical**
  - Strong negative characterisation of the institution itself (not just a specific person or meal) = institutional critique
- Exposing systemic failures through personal observation — **this is stance=critical, not buyin**
  - _"He took MC constantly, angkat bola'd superiors, and got promoted over people who worked hard"_ → **critical**
  - _"In war, conscripts like us want to fight alongside commanders like this encik. Not commanders who throw us under the bus to save their own skins."_ → **critical**
  - _"Imagine training for three months thinking you can serve the nation on the ground only to end up stuck in the same place"_ → **critical** (systemic misallocation — people deployed to administrative limbo instead of meaningful roles = institutional waste)
  - Revealing the system rewards wrong behaviour or misallocates people = institutional critique, even without explicit policy argument
  - Praise of a good individual used to contrast systemic bad behaviour: look at the PRIMARY message — if the point is exposing that bad commanders exist systemically, it is **critical** even when framed through positive contrast
- Demanding accountability from SAF/MINDEF (even in measured tone)
  - _"MINDEF has a responsibility to explain what happened"_ → **critical**
- Reform suggestions (generally critical)
- Framing NS as pointless/having no intrinsic value
  - _"If you don't intend to become a Singaporean citizen, there's no point in NS"_ → **critical** (stance) + **uncommitted** (buyin)

---

### NEUTRAL (stance default)
Use when no clear institutional stance. **Default when uncertain.**

- Pure personal experience with no policy dimension
- Factual, logistical, or jurisdictional content
- Personal venting about conditions without systemic critique
- Career advice about sign-on

## KEY EDGE CASES

### 1. Committed + Critical (both apply independently)
_"I serve proudly and gave my all, but NSF pay is a national disgrace"_
→ buyin=**committed (explicit)**, stance=**critical**

_"I'm generally supportive of defending our country spending 2yrs+IPPT+reservist, but the female MP telling us not to count dollars is a middle finger"_
→ buyin=**committed (explicit)**, stance=**critical**

_"In the future the good, the bad and the ugly of NS may be the memories I cherish, so I keep trying to just pause and absorb it... Yes, it's unfair that our other half doesn't serve."_
→ buyin=**committed (demonstrated)**, stance=**critical**

_"NS is necessary, but it definitely can be improved from the 'cannot be measured in dollars and cents' nonsense"_
→ buyin=**committed (demonstrated)**, stance=**critical**
Declarative "NS is necessary" = committed(d); "but [sharp institutional critique]" = critical — the "but" makes the critique the primary point, overriding the supportive opener.

_"I usually believe in 'every Singaporean son must serve', but forcing someone who left at age 4 to serve is illogical"_
→ buyin=**committed (demonstrated)**, stance=**critical**
Personal NS duty belief ("usually believe every son must serve") = committed(d); policy reform argument that follows does NOT cancel the buyin signal. Label each axis independently; do not let the supportive opener cancel the critical signal.

### 2. Analytical observer — no first-person stake
_"Any aggressor fighting NS conscripts is fighting the resource they want — beauty of NS"_
→ buyin=**neutral**, stance=**supportive**
No first-person stake = neutral buyin regardless of how positive the NS framing is.

### 3. "Women should serve NS too" — trimodal
- _"...it's only fair, men shouldn't bear this alone"_ → stance=**critical**
- _"...NS builds everyone's character"_ → stance=**supportive**
- _"...I don't see why I should serve if they don't"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**, stance=**critical**

### 4. Sign-on motivation matters
- _"I signed on for the pay and bonuses"_ → buyin=**neutral**
- _"I signed on because I believe in what we're defending"_ → buyin=**committed (explicit)**
- _"I signed on — money was part of it but I was genuinely interested in my vocation"_ → buyin=**committed (explicit)**
- _"Signing on gives a lucrative career and great work-life balance"_ → buyin=**neutral**
- _"I signed on because I needed money a couple of years back. Left after my bond was up."_ → buyin=**uncommitted**
- _"I plan to sign on for 5 years then leave once bond is up"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**
- _"Those who chiong 5 years finish the bond and zao are the winners"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**
  **Rule:** having done, planning to do, or endorsing the "sign on → finish bond → zao" strategy = uncommitted. The common thread is treating sign-on as a purely transactional contract to exit at the earliest opportunity, with no commitment beyond the bond. Money sign-on alone with no exit/zao framing → neutral.

### 5. ORD countdown
- _"69 days to ORD!!!! GET ME OUT"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**
- _"69 days to ORD, gonna miss the brothers though"_ → buyin=**neutral**
- _"ORD'd last month — served proudly and gave my best"_ → buyin=**committed (explicit)**

### 6. Values-based advice vs descriptive advice
- _"Learn to work together, help each other — that's what NS is about. Good luck!"_ → buyin=**committed (demonstrated)**
- _"BMT was easiest it'll get, now you give instructions — jia you!"_ → buyin=**neutral**

### 7. Conditional framing = uncommitted + critical
- _"If you don't intend to become a Singaporean citizen, there's no point in NS"_ → **uncommitted + critical**
- _"If being a spec isn't important to you, then OOC"_ → **uncommitted**

### 8. Systemic critique through personal story = stance critical
- _"He took MC constantly, angkat bola'd, and got promoted — it doesn't matter if you work hard"_
  → buyin=**neutral** (not about their own service), stance=**critical** (exposes system rewarding wrong behaviour)

### 9. Rebutting NS critics = supportive
- _"No one has ever died of hunger during NS — that narrative is wrong"_ → stance=**supportive**

### 10. Discouraging others = uncommitted
- _"Don't sign on, not worth it"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**
- _"Just chao keng lah, everyone does it"_ → buyin=**uncommitted**

---

## GENERAL RULES

1. **Default to neutral** on either axis when uncertain
2. **Do not infer uncommitted** from tone, conditions, or outcomes alone — need explicit disengagement language
3. **Do not infer committed** from admiring others or describing NS — author must express their OWN relationship
4. **Buyin and stance are independent** — label each axis separately
5. **Classify by substance, not sentiment** — cheerleading at the end doesn't override values-based content preceding it
6. **Rebuttal = supportive** — defending NS against critics counts as supportive stance even without explicit praise
7. **Conditional framing = uncommitted** — "no point unless X" signals NS has no intrinsic value
8. **Systemic observation = stance critical** — revealing the system rewards wrong behaviour is institutional critique on the STANCE axis; the buyin of someone describing this is typically neutral unless they express personal disengagement
